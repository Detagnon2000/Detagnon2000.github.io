About

From pure mathematics to models that matter.

I am a second-year PhD student in Mathematical Modeling in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at RIT, advised by Prof. Ernest Fokoué, and a member of the Data Science Research Group.

I trained in pure mathematics in Benin, in statistics for life sciences at ICMPA, and in mathematical sciences at AIMS Rwanda. Along the way I have measured the impact of malaria interventions, predicted HIV neutralization from viral sequences, and built an AI exam generator now in production in Benin.

The question behind all of it: what structure should a model preserve, and what do we gain when it does?

Category theory for machine learning

A model is a morphism; backpropagation is a functor into a category of lenses; a trainable layer is a parameterized lens. I study what this language makes precise, what it lets us prove once and reuse, and where it can change how models are designed.

HIV-1 envelope and neutralizing antibodies

Using long-read sequencing of the HIV-1 envelope, I applied TabPFN, a transformer-based model for tabular data, to predict autologous neutralization status, building the pipeline from feature engineering to evaluation. The resulting paper, on which I am third author, is under review.
